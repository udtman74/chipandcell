## Semiconductors: Memory Large Caps Decouple from Equipment and OSAT

Korea’s semiconductor space reflected a stark internal rotation over the past five trading days. While the median 20-day return stands at a steep -21.15%, front-line memory makers demonstrated relative stability this week. SK Hynix emerged as the sole gainer among major peers with a +0.63% return, and Samsung Electronics limited its decline to -1.15%. Even the third best performer of the week, materials supplier Dongjin Semichem, lost -6.72%, underscoring how concentrated the defensive flows were toward the two integrated device manufacturers (IDMs).

In contrast, the domestic equipment, testing, and outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) ecosystem bore the brunt of the sector's sell-off. Jusung Engineering was the week’s primary laggard, sinking -14.56%, followed closely by test socket specialist Leeno Industrial, which declined -13.26%. Hana Micron also saw heavy outflows, sliding -12.75%. The steep drawdown in these mid-cap supply chain names suggests that while global institutional capital remains willing to anchor memory exposure via SK Hynix and Samsung, appetite for downstream beta and capex-sensitive equipment suppliers has retrenched sharply amid broader supply-chain digestion concerns.

## Battery: Cathode High-Betas Drag Down the Supply Chain

The secondary battery sector extended its multi-week slump, with every tracked constituent ending the five-day period in negative territory. The median 20-day return sits at -7.76%, but weekly performance deteriorated significantly as sentiment around electric vehicle demand visibility worsened. Cell and specialty material producers proved relatively more resilient: Lotte Energy Materials held up best, dipping just -0.40%, followed by Nano Materials at -3.50% and cell manufacturer LG Energy Solution at -5.08%.

Conversely, the cathode active material (CAM) and upstream processing basket experienced severe de-rating. EcoPro BM dropped -12.77% over the five days, SKC fell -11.54%, and holding entity EcoPro retreated -10.80%. The sharp drawdowns across the EcoPro complex accounted for a significant portion of Friday's -1.62% decline in the KOSDAQ, where retail positioning in secondary battery names remains elevated. With raw material pricing volatility and inventory adjustments continuing to cloud margin recovery timelines, institutional flows favored the balance-sheet stability of Tier-1 cell makers over higher-beta chemical and cathode producers.
