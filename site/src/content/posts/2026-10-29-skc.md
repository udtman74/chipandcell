## Operational Headwinds and Portfolio Pressures

The velocity of SKC’s drawdown reflects its exposure to prolonged cyclical friction in its battery materials division, alongside valuation sensitivity in its advanced semiconductor packaging initiatives.

1. **Copper Foil Utilization and EV Demand Lag**  
SKC’s primary battery-facing subsidiary, SK nexilis, remains sensitive to operating leverage in its electrodeposited copper foil business. With tier-1 battery cell manufacturers managing conservative utilization rates and destocking inventory amid temperate global EV sales growth, copper foil shipments have faced persistent volume compression. Because copper foil production carries high fixed overhead—amplified by elevated domestic electricity tariffs in South Korea and the phased ramp-up of overseas capacity in Malaysia—utilization declines directly pressure operating margins.

2. **Unwinding of the Advanced Materials Premium**  
SKC's 60-day gain (+14.64%) previously reflected market enthusiasm for its non-battery transformation, namely semiconductor test sockets (ISC, acquired in 2023) and Absolics, its glass substrate subsidiary building capacity in Covington, Georgia. While glass substrates represent a secular theme for high-performance computing (HPC) packaging, commercial revenue generation remains a medium-to-long-term proposition. In risk-off tape—where foreign and institutional capital pulls back from high-multiple, high-beta assets—SKC's specialty semiconductor narrative has been insufficient to buffer the ongoing cash-flow drag from its legacy and battery materials segments.

3. **Capital Intensity and Financial Leverage**  
Unlike pure-play peers, SKC has undertaken multi-year portfolio restructuring, divesting legacy film and chemical assets to fund overseas capital expenditure. In an environment of delayed volume recovery from electric vehicle OEM supply chains, ongoing capital outlays weigh on debt metrics, heightening share-price volatility whenever sector-wide sentiment softens.
