## Company Drivers: Advanced Packaging and Laser Adoption

EO Technics' medium-term rally (+51.82% over 60 days) reflects structural shifts in semiconductor manufacturing architectures rather than broad beta expansion alone. As a supplier of laser-based semiconductor processing equipment—spanning laser markers, laser grooving, stealth dicing, and laser annealing tools—the company's addressable market is directly leveraged to advanced packaging and ultra-thin wafer handling.

1. **Shift to Laser-Based Die Separation:** The structural growth of High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) and multi-chip advanced packaging requires wafers to be ground to sub-30-micrometer thicknesses. Traditional mechanical blade saws cause micro-cracking and chipping at these dimensions. EO Technics has benefited from rising penetration of laser grooving (removing low-k dielectric materials before dicing) and stealth dicing (internal laser perforation), which reduce mechanical stress on delicate memory and logic dies.
2. **Expansion Beyond Marking:** Historically known for its high-margin laser markers—an area where the company holds dominant domestic market share—EO Technics has increasingly diversified revenue toward higher-ASP front-end and mid-end tools. Development cycles around laser-assisted bonding (LAB) and thermal laser annealing (which targets localized wafer heating without compromising heat-sensitive underlying layers) continue to position the firm as a primary beneficiary of next-generation packaging roadmaps at tier-1 Korean memory makers.
3. **Foreign Institutional Inflows:** The net purchase of 80,730 shares by foreign investors on a day when the broader KOSPI tumbled -2.62% highlights persistent offshore interest in Korean advanced packaging proxies, counterbalancing domestic institutional profit-taking.
