## Operational Exposure: Operating Leverage and OSAT Vulnerabilities

Hana Micron's sharp underperformance relative to both the broad market and the peer group median reflects the structural operating leverage inherent in its business model as South Korea’s primary memory-focused outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) provider. 

Unlike fabless design firms or diversified integrated device manufacturers (IDMs), Hana Micron’s earnings profile is heavily dictated by capacity utilization across its packaging and testing lines for its primary customers, SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics. Over recent years, Hana Micron committed heavy capital expenditure to expand dedicated back-end facilities—most notably through Hana Micron Vina in Vietnam to service multi-year memory packaging outsourcing commitments for SK Hynix. 

When demand signals weaken or domestic IDMs adjust production volumes, OSAT providers face an asymmetric margin squeeze. High fixed depreciation burdens from newly deployed cleanrooms and packaging equipment, combined with rising financing expenses from balance sheet expansion, penalize margins heavily when line utilization dips. The stock's 49.80% drawdown in 20 sessions suggests that markets are pricing in aggressive volume adjustments or deferred test allocations from key memory clients, exacerbating downward revisions in an already risk-off tape.

Furthermore, given the intensity of the broader market decline (KOSPI down 6.02% in a single session), aggressive forced deleveraging and collateral-driven liquidations across mid-cap technology holdings likely compounded the pressure on the name.
