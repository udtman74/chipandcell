## Structural Catalysts and Asset Repositioning

SKC's recent divergence from pure-play battery names reflects its evolving identity as a diversified advanced materials platform rather than a standalone secondary battery supplier:

1. **Cost Optimization in Copper Foil (SK nexilis):** While Korean cathode and cell manufacturers remain constrained by broader EV demand softness, SKC's copper foil subsidiary, SK nexilis, has been transitioning production toward its low-cost manufacturing footprint in Kota Kinabalu, Malaysia. By shifting production volumes away from higher-power-cost domestic lines in Jeongeup toward facilities benefiting from cheaper electricity, SK nexilis aims to reduce fixed-cost overhangs and margin dilution even as processing margins across the industry remain compressed.

2. **Advanced Packaging and Semiconductor Exposure:** Unlike pure-play EV battery peers, SKC’s valuation is increasingly supported by its semiconductor materials pipeline. Subsidiary Absolics completed its manufacturing facility in Covington, Georgia, to commercialize glass substrates designed for high-performance computing (HPC) and artificial intelligence packaging. As tier-1 chipmakers seek alternatives to organic substrates to manage thermal expansion and power delivery in large-die architectures, Absolics provides SKC with a growth narrative that is uncorrelated with battery cell destocking cycles. 

3. **Inorganic Semiconductor Contributions:** The operational integration of semiconductor test-socket maker ISC, acquired in 2023, continues to provide stable, higher-margin cash flows that partially insulate SKC’s operating profit from the volatility inherent in secondary battery raw materials and legacy chemical lines.

## Risks and Caveats

* **Copper Foil Industry Oversupply:** Chinese copper foil manufacturers continue to add capacity, keeping global utilization rates low and spot processing margins under pressure. If global battery demand softens further, SK nexilis may face delays in reaching targeted utilization rates in both Malaysia and its planned European footprint.
* **Commercialization Timelines for Glass Substrates:** Glass substrates represent an unproven mass-production technology. Customer qualification timelines, yield optimization hurdles, or delays in mass adoption by major semiconductor foundries could push back Absolics' timeline for revenue contribution.
* **Capital Intensity and Leverage:** SKC's aggressive capital expenditures across foreign copper foil plants and advanced semiconductor substrates have elevated debt levels and placed free cash flow under sustained pressure.
* **Technical Overhead:** Although SKC has cleared its MA20 and MA60, the stock remains roughly 43% below its 52-week high of ₩174,100, leaving substantial overhead supply that could limit upward momentum without sustained earnings recovery across both its battery and semiconductor segments.
