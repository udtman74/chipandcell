Korean equities faced a sharp end-of-week retreat on Friday, with the benchmark KOSPI shedding 1.76% and the junior KOSDAQ tumbling 1.95% as broader macro anxieties triggered late-week institutional de-risking. ## Semiconductor: Front-End Strength Trumps Back-End Plays

The semiconductor universe was defined by a widening divergence between front-end equipment/foundry plays and back-end assembly or design services. Memory heavyweight SK Hynix advanced +10.02% over the five-day period, supported by persistent foreign institutional inflows tracking high-bandwidth memory (HBM) supply tightness and favorable pricing dynamics for premium enterprise SSDs. 

Even stronger momentum emerged in specialized manufacturing and front-end equipment. DB HiTek emerged as the week’s top gainer, surging +17.69% amid growing market expectations of an inflection point in 8-inch foundry utilization rates and a bottoming out of power management IC (PMIC) destocking. Front-end tool supplier Jusung Engineering mirrored this strength, rising +17.22% as investors favored its proprietary atomic layer deposition (ALD) technology positioning ahead of next-generation DRAM architecture transitions. 

In stark contrast, downstream and peripheral names came under heavy selling pressure. Deposition peer Wonik IPS slid -3.92%, leading sector decliners as domestic fab-expansion capex remains heavily concentrated in high-end bottlenecks rather than broad greenfield capacity. Similarly, design house Gaonchips dropped -2.73%, and advanced outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) specialist Hana Micron fell -1.29%, reflecting profit-taking following previous speculative runs tied to domestic AI-accelerator initiatives.

## Battery: Foil Leaders Surge While Cell Makers Flatline

Korea’s secondary battery sector exhibited pronounced internal bifurcation, with upstream materials outperforming integrated cell manufacturers and cathode producers. Copper foil and advanced materials suppliers led the pack, highlighted by a +14.52% five-day leap in Lotte Energy Materials. Market participants rotated into the foil pure-play amid signs of stabilization in copper-spread margins and an expanding footprint with North American customers. SKC also gained +6.29%, bolstered by auxiliary catalysts around its semiconductor glass substrate venture, while battery-pack-holding conglomerate SK Innovation added +4.70% ahead of expected group-level balance sheet restructuring efforts.

Conversely, core midstream and chemical suppliers lagged significantly, pressured by ongoing uncertainty around global EV adoption rates and inventory rebalancing among major OEMs. Electrolyte additive maker Chunbo dropped -3.90%, remaining out of favor due to continued lithium salt price erosion and persistent margin compression. High-nickel cathode producer L&F slipped -1.07%, while blue-chip cell manufacturer Samsung SDI retreated -0.55%. The underperformance of Tier-1 cell makers underscores lingering investor caution regarding European EV subsidy rollbacks and mixed utilization rates, even as select upstream input providers stage technical rebounds.
