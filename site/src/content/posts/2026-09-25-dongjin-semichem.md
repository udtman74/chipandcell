## Fundamental and Operational Drivers

Dongjin Semichem’s operational leverage is tied to wafer starts and advanced packaging activity at its primary domestic clients, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix. The recent share-price firming coincides with several company-specific operational realities:

1. **Photolithography and Etch Chemistry Exposure:** Dongjin is the primary domestic producer of KrF and ArF immersion (ArFi) photoresists, alongside anti-reflective coatings (BARC) and ultra-pure process chemicals (such as sulfuric acid and thinners). As memory manufacturers ramp production of high-layer 3D NAND (exceeding 200 to 300 layers) and high-density DRAM nodes, total photolithography steps and wet-chemical consumption increase per wafer, directly benefiting Dongjin's core electronic materials volume.

While ArFi and KrF materials remain the dominant revenue drivers within the photoresist portfolio, progress in advanced node qualification provides high-margin optionality as leading-edge nodes scale.
3. **Overseas Capacity and Fab Alignment:** Dongjin’s multi-year capital deployment—notably its manufacturing subsidiary in Texas established to supply process chemicals to Samsung’s U.S. fab network—aligns the company with long-term geographic diversification, keeping market attention focused on capacity utilization ramps across late 2026.

## Risks and Caveats

* **Memory Utilization Volatility:** Dongjin's electronic materials division relies heavily on wafer run rates rather than new fab capital expenditures. Any selective wafer-start cuts or utilization pauses by major customers would immediately temper chemical replenishment rates.
* **Customer Concentration:** The company maintains heavy revenue dependency on Samsung Electronics. Pricing pressure during annual chemical contract renegotiations or delays in qualifying on next-generation sub-2nm/EUV layers could cap margin expansion.
* **Overseas Execution Timelines:** Slower-than-anticipated commercial production ramps at client facilities in North America could stretch pre-operating costs and weigh on return on invested capital (ROIC) in the near to medium term.
