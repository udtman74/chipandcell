## Fundamental Context and Operational Drivers

Leeno’s business model is tethered to the front end of chip verification rather than pure foundry or memory fabrication volume. As a premier global supplier of IC test sockets (particularly silicone rubber and spring-pin sockets) and secondary testing components (LEENO pins), the company generates demand primarily from R&D tape-outs and validation cycles for fabless and system-on-chip (SoC) designers.

Two fundamental factors help explain why institutional capital returned to the name:

1. **R&D Test Socket Exposure:** Unlike commoditized production test consumables, Leeno’s high-margin socket business expands whenever chip complexity increases—such as the integration of on-device neural processing units (NPUs), next-generation mobile application processors (APs), and automotive edge controllers. These advanced packaging architectures require higher pin density and narrower pitch, structural tailwinds that support Leeno's historically high operating margins (consistently exceeding 35–40%).
2. **Relative Valuation Catch-Up:** As capital rotated heavily across the Korean semiconductor complex over the past 20 sessions—pushing the sector median up 28.22%—Leeno had trailed that move until this session. Institutional desks appear to have treated Leeno's prior underperformance as an entry point into a cash-generative, unleveraged balance-sheet name with direct exposure to non-memory silicon design cycles.
